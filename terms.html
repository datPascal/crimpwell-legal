2. Not a medical device

Crimpwell is an education and self-management tool for climbers with finger injuries. It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any condition, and it does not replace a doctor, a hand specialist or a physiotherapist.

3. What the finger check does

The check compares your answers with injury patterns described in published research on climbing finger injuries. The result is the pattern that fits best. It is not a diagnosis. Only a clinician who examines your hand, often with an ultrasound or an X-ray, can tell what is injured. Crimpwell can be wrong.

4. How the plan works

The plan uses rehabilitation approaches from the research listed in the app under Sources. It asks about your pain before and after each session and uses fixed rules, shown in the app, to move on, hold or step back. It does not predict how long you will take to heal and does not promise a result. If your clinician tells you something different, follow your clinician.

5. When to get help

Don't wait for the app to tell you:

  • A finger that stays pale, white or blue once the hand is warm, or looks out of shape, needs emergency care now. Call 112 in the EU, 999 in the UK, 911 in the US and Canada.
  • A hot, red, fast-swelling finger or joint, a cut near it, or a fever needs a doctor today.
  • A pop followed by pain, or a finger you can't fully bend or straighten, needs a hand specialist or sports doctor within a few days.
  • Numbness or tingling needs a doctor within a few days.
  • A finger that isn't clearly better after two weeks, or gets worse, needs a check.

Crimpwell never locks you out of the app because of a warning sign. When one is present, it holds back the loading exercises until you tell it a clinician has checked the finger.

6. Your responsibility

You decide whether and how to train. Stop any exercise that causes sharp pain, numbness or tingling. Training an injured finger carries a risk of making it worse.
