Privacy policy

Last updated: 30 October 2026

Short version. Crimpwell keeps what you tell it on your phone. It has no account, no server, no analytics and no ads. I, the developer, never receive your answers, your pain scores or your plan.

Who is responsible

Pascal Lindenau, Forddamm 7, 12107 Berlin, Germany. Email: pascallindenau@googlemail.com. I am the controller under the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) for the little personal data that reaches me, which is described below.

What Crimpwell stores on your phone

  • your answers to the finger check, such as which finger hurts, when it started and which grips hurt;
  • the result of the check and the care advice that came with it;
  • your climbing style, training days, equipment and goal;
  • your morning checks, pain scores, session logs, plan changes, appointment dates and notes;
  • your settings, such as reminder times, and whether you own Pro.

This data stays in the app's private storage on the phone. Other apps can't read it, and Crimpwell does not upload it anywhere. Injury and pain data are health data under Article 9 GDPR, which is one reason the app keeps them on the device. If you back up your phone to iCloud or a computer, the backup can include Crimpwell's data. Apple's terms cover those backups.

What reaches me

Nothing from inside the app. Crimpwell has no networking code of its own and no third-party code that sends data.

Purchases

Crimpwell Pro is a one-time purchase through the App Store. Apple processes the payment. I never see your card, your name or your Apple Account. The app asks Apple whether you own Pro and keeps the answer on the phone so Pro keeps working offline.

Reminders

If you allow notifications, Crimpwell schedules reminders on the phone itself, such as a daily session reminder. No server sends them. You can turn them off in the app or in iOS Settings.

Data from Apple

If you chose to share analytics or crash reports with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may give me aggregated usage numbers and crash reports with device model and iOS version. I can't link them to anything you entered in Crimpwell. If you test Crimpwell through TestFlight, Apple also shares the build you installed, crashes, feedback you choose to send and, if I invited you by email, your name and email address. I use this only to fix bugs (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Links

Some screens link to scientific papers and to Apple's pages. Those sites have their own privacy practices. Opening a link leaves Crimpwell.

Email

If you email me, I use your address and message to answer you (Article 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR). I delete the conversation when it is no longer needed, unless the law requires me to keep it.

Deleting your data

Open the You tab and tap Delete all my data. Deleting the app also removes everything it stored on the phone.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask me for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal data I hold about you, restrict or object to processing, and ask for a copy in a portable format. Write to pascallindenau@googlemail.com. You can also complain to a data protection authority. The one responsible for me is the Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit, Alt-Moabit 59-61, 10555 Berlin.

Children

Crimpwell is made for adult climbers. A climber under 16 who uses the check is told to see a doctor before training the finger. Because the app sends nothing to me, I don't collect data from anyone, children included.

Changes

If Crimpwell starts handling data differently, I will update this page and the date at the top before that version ships.

Crimpwell is not a medical device. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.
